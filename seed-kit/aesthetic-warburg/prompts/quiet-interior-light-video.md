# Quiet Interior Light — moving image (静室之光作为长镜头)

## Subject
A single room at early morning, window on the left wall.
A figure enters, performs one unhurried domestic action (setting down a pack, pouring water, opening a letter, standing at the window), and the camera does not cut.
Duration: one continuous take of 90–180 seconds.
Optional: the figure leaves frame and the room is held alone for the final third of the shot.

## Style
Vermeer 1657 (Milkmaid) window-light discipline as the origin grammar / Hammershøi c.1900 (Interior) as the existential emptied variant / Deakins 2019 (1917 farmhouse dawn) as the cinematographic contemporary node — cold blue dawn, handheld steadiness, survivor framing.
The lineage refuses montage; this is the Tarkovsky-Deakins-Hammershøi axis, not the Nolan axis.

## Composition
Static tripod or micro-slow dolly-in (≤ 2cm/sec). Window at left third. Figure staged at right-center, three-quarter turned to the light.
Horizontal baseline held across the take — no tilted angles, no Dutch tilts.
Color key: cold-blue dawn from window, warm bone-white wall interior, one ochre accent (garment, wood). Film grain present but quiet.

## Motion
Long take. Time dilation, not stillness — the viewer must feel time pass unbroken.
Figure's motion is breath-paced, not choreographed. The camera breathes with the figure or does not move at all.
No whip pans, no jump cuts, no speed ramps. Reference: Deakins's unbroken farmhouse dawn in 1917; Tarkovsky's 4-minute domestic takes.

## Notes
Do NOT generate: fast cutting, music-video pacing, aerial drone swoops, hyperreal slow-mo water droplets, split-screen, montage compression, timelapse sunrise, whispered voiceover, a dramatic score swell. This Pathosformel requires the audience to wait inside the image — if it hurries, it is no longer this form.
