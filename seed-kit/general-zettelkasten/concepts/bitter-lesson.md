# Bitter Lesson

The recurring pattern Sutton identifies across 70 years of AI research: approaches that bake human knowledge about *how the problem should be solved* win short-term (they feel ingenious), plateau medium-term (the encoded knowledge becomes a ceiling), and lose long-term (compute scales past them). The two methods that absorb compute gracefully are [[search-and-learning]]; everything else is a short-term optimization that Moore's law eventually overtakes.

Called "bitter" because the successful approach keeps being the one researchers emotionally resisted — their domain expertise turned out to be the obstacle, not the asset.
