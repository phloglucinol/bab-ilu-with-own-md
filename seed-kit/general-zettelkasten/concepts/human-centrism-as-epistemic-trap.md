# Human-centrism as Epistemic Trap

The claim that human-centric framings — "how *I* think the problem should be solved," "what *my* intuitions say is the explanation" — are systematic epistemic blockers, not neutral starting points. Two independent sources converge on this at different layers:

- **Methodology layer**: Sutton's [[bitter-lesson]] — encoding human domain knowledge into AI systems wins short-term and loses long-term because Moore's law keeps widening the compute gap with [[scaling-hypothesis]]-compatible approaches
- **Cognition layer**: Varol (ch. 1, *Dancing with Uncertainty*) — we fill epistemic voids with stories (Kahneman) and mistake self-certainty for knowledge (Boorstin), so the "obvious" interpretation becomes the thing we can't see past

The value of pairing them: neither is sufficient alone. Sutton tells you not to bake your mental model into the system; Varol tells you not to trust your mental model in the first place. The cluster sits at [[epistemic-humility-under-uncertainty]].
