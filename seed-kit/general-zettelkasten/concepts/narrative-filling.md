# Narrative Filling

The mechanism Varol surfaces via Kahneman — but labelled here as a **paraphrase**, not a direct quote. Varol's chapter frames the move as: we cannot live in a permanent state of doubt, so when we face an epistemic void we produce a causal story that closes the gap, and the story starts feeling like evidence simply because nothing else is available.

**Attribution honesty** (round-4 audit correction): Kahneman writes about this mechanism across *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (2011) — his terms are "WYSIATI" (what you see is all there is) and the "narrative fallacy" (which Kahneman credits Taleb for naming, in *The Black Swan*, 2007). The *exact* phrasing used in this file's previous revision ("We cannot live in a permanent state of doubt…") could not be verified against Kahneman's published text, so it's treated as Varol's gloss rather than a direct quote. This concept-under-this-slug names the *mechanism* both authors circle, not a specific phrase.

Why it matters: the story doesn't need to be true to be sticky. Conspiracy theories, retrospective "I always knew" narratives, cargo-cult best practices — these are all narrative-fillings. Recognizing the move in oneself is an act of [[uncertainty-literacy]].
