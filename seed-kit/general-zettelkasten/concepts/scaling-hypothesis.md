# Scaling Hypothesis

The claim that progress in a domain comes primarily from graceful absorption of more computation, rather than from increasingly clever encodings of domain knowledge. **The phenomenon** is what Sutton diagnoses in *The Bitter Lesson* (2019): the lesson of 70 years of AI history that the two primitive operations that scale arbitrarily with compute — [[search-and-learning]] — are the only durable bets; anything that depends on a specific encoding of how the problem should be solved eventually plateaus. **The phrase itself** ("scaling hypothesis") was coined later, most prominently by Gwern Branwen's 2020 essay *The Scaling Hypothesis*, not by Sutton. The concept-under-this-slug names the phenomenon, not the phrase, so it anchors to Sutton as the primary source.
