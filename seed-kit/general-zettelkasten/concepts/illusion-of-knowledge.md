# Illusion of Knowledge

Boorstin's formulation (quoted in Varol ch.1): *"The greatest obstacle to discovery is not ignorance — it is the illusion of knowledge."* The exact published phrase is "illusion of knowledge" — this file's slug and title preserve Boorstin's term verbatim. Prior revisions used "illusion of expertise," which was a Bab-ilu-author reframing, not Boorstin's language; round-4 audit correctly flagged the attribution drift.

The claim: the real epistemic blocker is not missing facts, it's the *feeling of already knowing* that prevents the question from being asked. Confidence substitutes for evidence, familiarity masquerades as understanding, and the conversation forecloses before curiosity can enter.

Distinct from ordinary error: ordinary error is a wrong answer that's correctable by new information. The illusion of knowledge is a *posture* — it resists new information because admitting the gap would undermine the self-presentation.
