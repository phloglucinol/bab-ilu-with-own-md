# 通过写作思考 — Progressive Summary Template

## Source
- Book / paper / conversation being summarized: *Building a Second Brain* — Tiago Forte (2022)
- Vault anchor: [[progressive-summarization]]

## Layer 1 — Bold key sentences (raw highlighting)
When reading the source, bold the sentences that answer the question [[gap-as-unwritten-note]] is asking: where does the unfinished thinking live, and how do you surface it?

Not "motivating" sentences — structural ones. The test: does this sentence explain *how* writing transforms the thinker, or does it merely assert that it does?

Example pull-quote from Forte:
> "**Progressive Summarization is not about remembering more; it is about being forced to decide, each time you return, what still matters**."

A second candidate:
> "**The gap between what you have highlighted and what you can explain is the exact size of your misunderstanding.**"

## Layer 2 — Bold fragments inside Layer 1
From the bolded sentences above, pick 3–5 fragments that carry the conceptual weight. Drop connective tissue.

- **forced to decide, each time you return**
- **what still matters**
- **gap between what you have highlighted and what you can explain**
- **exact size of your misunderstanding**

## Layer 3 — One-sentence thesis
Writing is not the output of understanding but its engine: each return visit to a [[progressive-summarization]] layer forces the kind of judgment that converts [[gap-as-unwritten-note]] into legible thought.

## Back-references (what this unlocks)
- [[zettelkasten-mechanics]] — explains why the atomic note constraint exists: writing small forces the decision that Layer 2 demands
- [[bloom-taxonomy]] — Layer 3 here operates at Bloom's "evaluate" level; tracing which layer maps to which Bloom level reveals the cognitive depth of a summarization pass

## Traps
- Treating Layer 1 as a reading highlight dump rather than a targeted answer to a specific question
- Writing Layer 3 before Layer 2 is fully compressed — the one-sentence thesis should feel earned, not imposed
- Confusing "what I found interesting" with "what carries conceptual weight"; interest is personal, weight is structural
