# Zettelkasten 机制 — Progressive Summary Template

## Source
- Book / paper / conversation being summarized: *How to Take Smart Notes* — Sönke Ahrens (2017)
- Vault anchor: [[source-vs-literature-note]]

## Layer 1 — Bold key sentences (raw highlighting)
When reading the source, bold the sentences that answer the question [[atomic-note-principle]] is asking: what makes a note a *unit* of thinking rather than a unit of storage?

Not "interesting" sentences — load-bearing ones. The test: if you removed this sentence, would a claim downstream collapse?

Example pull-quote from Ahrens:
> "**Every note is only as valuable as its connections to other notes**; the slip-box is not an archive but a thinking partner."

A second candidate:
> "**Write exactly one thing per note**, not one topic — a topic can hold infinite ideas, a thought cannot."

## Layer 2 — Bold fragments inside Layer 1
From the bolded sentences above, pick 3–5 fragments that carry the conceptual weight. Drop connective tissue.

- **connections to other notes**
- **not an archive but a thinking partner**
- **exactly one thing per note**
- **a thought cannot** (hold infinite ideas)

## Layer 3 — One-sentence thesis
The Zettelkasten generates understanding not by accumulating text but by forcing every note to prove its right to exist through [[atomic-note-principle]] and to earn its place through [[link-as-knowledge-unit]].

## Back-references (what this unlocks)
- [[thinking-through-writing]] — the mechanics here explain *why* writing a permanent note changes the thinker, not just the vault
- [[permanent-note]] — Layer 3 here is the specification for what a permanent note must achieve

## Traps
- Layer 1 that bolds "interesting" passages instead of load-bearing claims (decoration, not extraction)
- Layer 3 that restates the author's conclusion instead of synthesizing across the cluster
- Treating this template as a one-pass exercise — Forte's method requires re-reading to promote fragments
