## Example 1 — Friedrich, *Der Mönch am Meer* / *Monk by the Sea* (1808–1810)

### Source

| Field | Value |

| Artist | Caspar David Friedrich |
| Year | 1808–1810 |
| Medium | Oil on canvas |
| Dimensions | 110 × 171.5 cm |
| Repository | Alte Nationalgalerie Berlin, Inv. A I 486 |
| Vault anchor | `[[friedrich-monk-by-the-sea]]` |
| Artist anchor | `[[caspar-david-friedrich]]` |

---

### Pre-iconographic layer (Panofsky I)

*What a viewer with no cultural knowledge sees — shapes, surfaces,
tonal relations, spatial divisions, motion or its absence. No named
objects, no identified persons, no genre conventions.*

The canvas is divided horizontally into three bands of sharply unequal
weight. The lowest band is a narrow strip of pale ochre-grey,
occupying roughly the bottom eighth of the picture plane. Above it,
occupying perhaps a quarter of the total height, a dark tonal band
runs edge to edge — not quite black, not quite brown, carrying
occasional surface variation but no legible differentiation of texture.
The remaining two-thirds of the canvas, from mid-height to the top
edge, is a mass of pale grey-white with irregular tonal modulation
moving lighter toward the upper right quadrant and darkening near the
horizon line at center.

A single vertical element interrupts the lowest band: a dark mark,
roughly the proportion of a standing person, placed slightly left of
center. The mark is small enough that it reads as a disturbance in the
horizontal rhythm rather than a primary subject. It does not move
toward either edge; it simply stands. There is no second vertical mark
anywhere in the composition, and no convergence lines that would
produce depth. The three horizontal bands press against one another
with no atmospheric bridging between them. The eye cannot settle
because there is no compositional incident to settle on — the canvas
refuses the middle-distance pull that European landscape convention
relies on. Stillness is not peaceful here; it is enforced.

---

### Iconographic layer (Panofsky II)

*What identified objects and established conventions mean within their
tradition. Cultural knowledge is now permitted; authority vocabularies
are cited.*

The three horizontal zones are identifiable as sand, sea, and sky —
the beach panorama convention of North German coastal painting, a
format Friedrich was developing in Dresden contemporaneously with
*Kreidefelsen auf Rügen* (c. 1818). The small dark figure is a
Rückenfigur (back-turned figure), an iconographic device Friedrich
systematized across his career: the viewer's surrogate, positioned
facing away so that we cannot read a face but are invited to project
our own orientation into the gaze. This is distinct from the
traditional staffage figure of 17th-century Dutch and Flemish
landscape, which exists to indicate scale without demanding
identification. Friedrich's Rückenfigur demands it.

The figure's dress — dark coat, the silhouette suggesting clerical
or monastic habit — locates this image within a post-Napoleonic
Protestant vocabulary of solitary witness, though the identification
as specifically monastic is contested by scholars (the title was
added later). The extreme emptying of the composition — almost no
sky incident, no trees, no architectural element — violates the
conventions of *Heroic Landscape* (a recognized genre; see AAT
`300015636`, landscape painting) and *Capriccio*, both of which
provide visual incident to mediate the human/nature confrontation.
Friedrich removes the mediation.

Iconclass notation `25H11` (seascape) partially covers the formal
subject; `25H1121` (coastal landscape with breaking waves) captures it more precisely.
The Rückenfigur convention itself has no dedicated Iconclass notation
— it operates as a compositional device cutting across subject
categories. Wikidata `Q151049` (Caspar David Friedrich) and `Q1128454`
(*Monk by the Sea*) anchor artist and work to institutional records.
The Alte Nationalgalerie acquisition record provides the primary
provenance anchor.

---

### Iconological layer (Panofsky III)

*The symbolic values — what worldview this composition assumes, who it
addresses, and what it cannot say. Honest about uncertainty.*

The composition's refusal of mediation is the iconological claim.
Post-Napoleonic German Romanticism required a new visual grammar for
the individual's relationship to the infinite, having discredited the
Enlightenment's confident ratio and the church's intercessory
architecture as mediators. Friedrich's Rückenfigur says: the encounter
with the sublime happens without institutional scaffolding, and it
happens in silence so total that speech would dissolve. This is not
*about* a monk; the monastic habit is a visual heuristic for "a person
who has already surrendered worldly certitude." The actual claim is
about the subject position of modern European interiority: alone,
facing something vast, without language.

Who is excluded: this composition assumes a bourgeois male Protestant
viewer for whom solitary encounter with nature is a vehicle of
transcendence — not a women's indoor tradition, not a communal
devotional tradition, not a labor tradition where nature is worked
rather than contemplated. The figure's passivity is iconologically
legible only to a viewer who has already absorbed the Romantic
equation: stillness = spiritual capacity. Caution: attributing
"existential loneliness" to this figure reads 20th-century categories
backward onto 1808. Friedrich's loneliness was Romantic-theological,
not secular-psychological.

---

### Pathosformel attribution (Warburg)

**Affective posture (one sentence):** A single figure, back to the
viewer, arrested before a void disproportionately larger than
themselves — posture of the subject who witnesses rather than acts,
whose witness is the subject's entire claim on significance.

**Panel:** `[[sublime-solitude-landscape]]`

**Nachleben trail (cross-media siblings in the vault):**

- `[[friedrich-monk-by-the-sea]]` (1808–1810, German Romantic
  painting) — the posture's documented European origin node
- `[[tarkovsky-stalker-zone]]` (1979, Soviet cinema) — the
  Rückenfigur's affective posture survives here, but rotated 180°:
  Friedrich's figure faces the void with his back to us; Tarkovsky's
  figures move *into* the void with their backs to the camera and their
  faces hidden by forward motion. Same bearing, different
  directionality — threshold crossed rather than threshold witnessed.
- Denis Villeneuve, *Blade Runner 2049* (2017, Hollywood) — `[PROPOSAL:
  blade-runner-2049-orange-void]` — Officer K alone in the orange-dust
  wasteland, back to camera, facing a horizon that offers no resolution.
  The Rückenfigur posture survives from German Romanticism through Soviet
  late modernism into Hollywood post-human science fiction. The rupture
  in 2049 is not existential-theological but ontological: K does not
  know if he is capable of transcendence at all.

Note: the 1808 → 1979 → 2017 trail crosses German Romanticism, Soviet
late modernism, and Hollywood genre cinema — it qualifies as Nachleben
rather than lineage. No direct citation chain exists; the posture
transmits through the visual unconscious, shaped by 19th-century
landscape photography and the Rückenfigur's penetration into
pedagogical art-historical imagery.

### Back-references

1. **Friedrich 1808 → Tarkovsky 1979, posture rotation.** The
   Rückenfigur in *Monk by the Sea* holds still; the viewer contemplates
   the void through the figure's immobility. In `[[tarkovsky-stalker-zone]]`,
   the Zone sequences invert this: the three figures *enter* the void,
   their backs to the camera as they move forward. Friedrich's witness
   posture becomes Tarkovsky's threshold-crossing posture — the affective
   load (encounter with something that exceeds symbolic order) is
   identical; the motor disposition is opposite. This inversion is the
   specific Nachleben claim, not a vague "both involve nature."

2. **Friedrich 1808 → `[[quiet-interior-light]]` pathosformel, a
   structural contrast.** Both `[[sublime-solitude-landscape]]` and
   `[[quiet-interior-light]]` are panels in which the human figure is
   deprioritized against an environment. But the directional logic is
   opposed: the Rückenfigur faces *outward* toward an unbounded void;
   `[[contained-silence]]` in Vermeer/Hammershøi pulls *inward* toward
   a domestic enclosure. Same figure-reduction operation; opposite
   spatial orientation. The two panels are the best evidence that
   Pathosformel is positional rather than thematic.

3. **Friedrich's post-Napoleonic rupture vs. Villeneuve's post-human
   rupture.** The same affective posture passes through two structurally
   different historical crises: in 1808, the Romantic subject has
   survived Enlightenment disenchantment and Napoleonic war, and faces
   a nature that no longer contains providential meaning; in 2049
   (proposed `blade-runner-2049-orange-void`), the post-human subject
   faces a nature that has been chemically destroyed. The Pathosformel
   is the same; the historical content of the "void" has changed from
   theological absence to ecological aftermath.

---

### Traps avoided

1. **Did not attribute "loneliness" or "mourning" as the iconological
   content.** The figure's back is withheld, which means its emotional
   state is *structurally undetermined* at Layer I. Reading "mourning"
   or "loneliness" into the composition imports 20th-century secular-
   psychological categories. Friedrich's visual rhetoric is Romantic-
   theological: the figure is a *witness*, not a sufferer. The
   distinction matters because the Nachleben trail to Tarkovsky and
   Villeneuve only holds if the Pathosformel is posture-based, not
   mood-based.

2. **Did not treat the Nachleben trail as conscious influence.** The
   phrasing above is "the Rückenfigur posture *survives into*
   Tarkovsky's Zone sequences," not "Tarkovsky *quoted* Friedrich."
   There is no evidence Tarkovsky was thinking of this painting.
   Nachleben transmits through the visual unconscious — through 19th-
   century landscape photography, pedagogical art-history reproduction,
   and the entire visual grammar that preceded Tarkovsky's training.

3. **Did not inflate the motif list to meet coverage targets.** The
   composition contains three identifiable motifs that appear in the
   seed-kit. Adding `[[water-as-memory-surface]]` or
   `[[atmospheric-light-dissolving-form]]` would require arguing that
   the sea in this painting functions as a memory medium or that
   atmospheric dissolution is a compositional device here — neither is
   defensible at Layer I. Three accurate motifs outrank five speculative
   ones.

## Example 2 — Tarkovsky, *Stalker* — Zone sequences (1979)

### Source

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Work title | *Stalker* — Zone sequences |
| Director | Andrei Tarkovsky |
| Year | 1979 |
| Medium | Film (35mm, Mosfilm production) |
| Duration | approx. 163 min total; Zone sequences begin ~35 min |
| Vault anchor | `[[tarkovsky-stalker-zone]]` |
| Director anchor | `[[andrei-tarkovsky]]` |
| Source text | `[PROPOSAL: roadside-picnic-strugatsky]` (Strugatsky brothers, *Roadside Picnic*, 1972; dangling ref in seed-kit) |

---

### Pre-iconographic layer (Panofsky I)

*What a viewer with no cultural knowledge sees — shapes, tonal
relations, spatial divisions, motion and duration. No named objects,
no genre conventions, no narrative identifications.*

The image surface transitions from high-contrast silver-black
(the scenes before the Zone are shot in desaturated sepia) into a
warm green-amber tonal register as the Zone sequences begin. This
shift is not announced; it happens across an edit and can register as
a changed light source before it registers as a changed world. Once
inside this register, the frames are dense with wet horizontal
textures: grasses, reeds, shallow water surfaces that reflect the
sky without resolving it into a clean mirror. Depth is available but
resists use — a viewer's eye moves into the frame through layered
planes of organic material, but each plane obscures rather than
clarifies the next.

Three dark vertical figures move through these planes at varying
depths. They do not walk at even pace; they pause, crouch, move
laterally, pause again. The camera does not follow them with
conventional coverage logic — it holds a position for a duration
that exceeds narrative justification. Several sequences run past four
minutes without an edit. A viewer habituated to commercial cinema
tempo will experience this duration as an error before experiencing
it as a choice. Sound is continuous: water, wind, material creaking —
ambient rather than scored. The figures' faces are occasionally
visible but not emphasized; the camera does not privilege facial
expression over the relationship between bodies and environment.
Objects are present — rusted metal, collapsed structures, rope, a
glass — but their function is not demonstrated narratively; they
occupy space without instruction.

---

### Iconographic layer (Panofsky II)

*What identified objects and conventions mean in their tradition.
Authority vocabularies cited.*

The Zone is iconographically a post-industrial wasteland: collapsed
structures, rust, overgrown industrial detritus, standing water.
These objects index a specific Soviet visual vocabulary — the
aftermath of heavy industry, the abandoned factory or military site
(compare the iconography of the Soviet "zone" in photography of
Chernobyl's exclusion zone, though Chernobyl was 1986, seven years
after this film; the visual vocabulary preceded the historical event).
The spatial condition is the Limen (threshold): a bounded territory
that operates under suspended or altered rules, crossed by initiatory
rites. Victor Turner's anthropological limen (1969) and Arnold van
Gennep's *rites de passage* (1909) map directly onto the Zone's
spatial logic — the three figures are in a liminal condition between
the ordinary world (indexical, rule-governed) and an inner sanctum
(the Room) that remains off-screen.

The Stalker figure — the guide who knows the Zone's informal
protocols without understanding them scientifically — maps onto an
iconographic tradition of the *psychopomp*: Hermes/Mercury leading
souls across a threshold (Iconclass `92B23`). The film makes no
explicit mythological reference; the convergence is structural, not
decorative. The long-take method (镜头不切) invokes no specific
iconographic tradition; it is a formal device that performs
temporality as subject (see `[[long-take-temporal-dilation]]`).
The water surfaces throughout the Zone activate an established
iconographic association: water as memory, reflection as temporal
superposition (see `[[water-as-memory-surface]]`; the objects
submerged in Zone water — a syringe, a calendar, a revolver — are
the film's most compressed iconographic statement).

IMDb identifier: `tt0079944` (*Stalker*, 1979, Mosfilm). No AAT term
directly covers "liminal spatial zone" as a motif — the closest is
`300004914` (threshold, architectural element), which covers the
literal rather than the experiential phenomenon.

---

### Iconological layer (Panofsky III)

*The symbolic values — what worldview this composition assumes, and
against whom. Honest about uncertainty.*

The Zone's iconological claim is that the modern subject cannot
distinguish between what they believe (the Zone has powers) and what
they project (the Zone reflects desire). The Room, said to grant
one's innermost wish, is the film's trap: the Stalker believes, the
Writer doubts instrumentally, the Professor intends to destroy. None
of them reaches the Room. The Zone refuses the film's ending, and the
film refuses to resolve whether the refusal is the Zone's agency or
the figures' incapacity.

This composition assumes a viewer formed by late Soviet disillusionment
with both technocratic materialism (the Professor) and aesthetic-
intellectual irony (the Writer) — a viewer for whom neither science
nor literature has delivered on its promise of meaning-making. The
Zone is the visual proposition that neither framework is adequate;
what remains is something pre-rational and spatially embodied —
faith, or its secular remnant. Against whom: the Soviet ideological
apparatus that required either scientific optimism (materialism can
explain and control the Zone) or skeptical debunking (the Zone is
superstition). Tarkovsky's Zone refuses both exits.

Caution: reading the Zone as a straight religious allegory (the Room
= grace; the Stalker = Christ-figure) is available but
overcommitted. The film's irreducibility to any single iconological
frame is itself part of the iconological content — an image that
remains undecipherable within the symbolic order it enters.

---

### Pathosformel attribution (Warburg)

**Affective posture (one sentence):** Three figures moving through a
space that watches them back — the Zone is not a setting but a
respondent, and the posture of the figures is that of people who
cannot tell if they are the subjects or the objects of an attention
they cannot locate.

**Panel:** `[[liminal-threshold-zone]]`

**Nachleben trail (cross-media siblings in the vault):**

- `[[tarkovsky-stalker-zone]]` (1979, Soviet cinema) — the panel's
  anchor work; the Zone as the first fully articulated cinematic
  Pathosformel of the space-that-responds
- `[[friedrich-monk-by-the-sea]]` (1808–1810, German Romantic
  painting) — inverted sibling: Friedrich's figure faces the void
  from the near shore, refusing entry; Tarkovsky's figures are already
  inside. The affective load (encounter with something that exceeds
  symbolic order) is shared; the spatial relation is reversed.
- Alex Garland, *Annihilation* (2018, Hollywood) — `[PROPOSAL:
  annihilation-shimmer-zone]` — the Shimmer carries the same Pathosformel
  as Tarkovsky's Zone: a bounded territory with suspended physics, entered
  by a small group, that responds to the entrants' internal states rather
  than to external physical law. The rupture differs: Tarkovsky's Zone is
  post-industrial and post-theological; Garland's Shimmer is post-
  biological and ecological. The Pathosformel — space-as-respondent —
  survives the 39-year gap and the cultural translation from Soviet
  cinema to Hollywood science fiction.

Note: the 1979 → 2018 trail crosses Soviet late modernism and Hollywood
science fiction, satisfying the cross-cultural Nachleben requirement.
The trail would be strengthened by a non-Western member; the current
vault is missing this, and `cross_tradition_gap` should be flagged on
the `[[liminal-threshold-zone]]` panel until a non-Western work is
ingested (candidates: Naomi Kawase's spatial cinema; the *mo no aware*
tradition in Mizoguchi; the *ma* / negative space concept in
Japanese architecture). Flag: `cross_tradition_gap: true`.

### Back-references

1. **Tarkovsky 1979 → Friedrich 1808, the threshold inversion.** In
   `[[friedrich-monk-by-the-sea]]`, the Rückenfigur stands at the edge
   of the void and holds still — the posture is witness, not crossing.
   In the Zone sequences, the figures have already crossed; the threshold
   is behind them, not in front. The affective Pathosformel (encounter
   with something that exceeds symbolic order) is shared, but Friedrich's
   posture is threshold-adjacent and Tarkovsky's is threshold-interior.
   This distinction matters for the Mnemosyne Atlas panel separation:
   `[[sublime-solitude-landscape]]` and `[[liminal-threshold-zone]]`
   are not the same panel, though both handle the edge of the symbolic
   order. The difference is directionality.

2. **Tarkovsky 1979 → `[[water-as-memory-surface]]` → Turner 1844.**
   The Zone's shallow water, with its submerged objects and unstable
   reflections, shares a motif with Turner's *Rain, Steam and Speed*
   (`[[turner-rain-steam-speed]]`): in both, water functions as the
   visual medium for temporal superposition — past and present made
   coplanar. But the emotional valence differs: Turner's water is
   modernity's velocity (speed dissolves form); Tarkovsky's water is
   modernity's wreckage (objects that should have been cleared away
   remain, half-submerged). Same motif, opposed historical mood. The
   motif `[[water-as-memory-surface]]` is the vault's evidence that
   Pathosformel and mood are separable.

3. **The Zone's spatial logic anticipates `[[quiet-interior-light]]`
   by inversion.** `[[quiet-interior-light]]` (Vermeer → Hammershøi →
   Deakins) generates a Pathosformel of enclosed space that contains
   rather than threatens. The Zone generates the opposite: a space that
   is formally enclosed (bounded by the perimeter) but experientially
   unbounded (rules suspended, depth indeterminate). Both panels
   deprioritize human figures in favor of spatial atmosphere — but one
   encloses and reassures; the other opens and destabilizes. Reading
   these two panels against each other reveals that the
   "space-as-protagonist" operation is available to opposite affective
   registers.

---

### Traps avoided

1. **Did not read the Zone as a "symbol" with a fixed referent.** The
   iconological layer above marks the Zone's undecidability as itself
   an iconological datum, not as a failure of analysis. Forcing a
   single allegorical reading (Zone = God / Zone = unconscious / Zone =
   Soviet surveillance state) closes what the image deliberately holds
   open. The Warburg method requires naming the affective posture;
   it does not require resolving the narrative.

2. **Did not treat Nachleben as genre influence.** The phrasing above is
   "the liminal-zone Pathosformel *survives into* Garland's *Annihilation*,"
   not "Garland was influenced by Tarkovsky" (though he was; he has said
   so in interviews). Nachleben as a method applies independently of
   whether conscious influence can be documented. The structural
   convergence of the Pathosformel is the analytic datum.

3. **Did not promote the panel before it meets the threshold.** The
   `[[liminal-threshold-zone]]` panel has one confirmed work member in
   the vault. The examples above propose two additional works but mark
   both as `[PROPOSAL]`. The panel is flagged as below `cluster_member_min`
   until confirmed file creation. Promoting it as a full panel now would
   violate the lens's `cluster_member_min: 3` threshold.

## How these examples calibrate Claude's behavior

When a new aesthetic arrives under this lens, follow this discipline:

1. **Write Layer I first, in isolation, before naming anything.** The
   pre-iconographic layer must be written as if the viewer has no
   vocabulary for what they see — shapes, tonal relations, spatial
   divisions, durations. If a named object or person appears in Layer I,
   you have already leaked Layer II. Both examples above draft Layer I
   before any named objects are introduced.

2. **Layer II cites authority anchors, not taste.** Iconographic content
   is what the tradition names, not what seems evocative. Cite AAT terms,
   Iconclass notations, Wikidata QIDs. Where no authority term exists, say
   so and propose a descriptive label rather than borrowing a term that
   does not fit (see the `aat_id_uncertainty` pattern in both examples).

3. **Layer III names the worldview assumption and its exclusions.** The
   iconological claim is not "what this painting is about" — it is "what
   view of the world this composition takes for granted, and who is not
   addressed." Both examples above include a sentence identifying who is
   excluded from the composition's assumed viewer.

4. **The Pathosformel is a posture sentence, not a theme sentence.**
   "A single figure facing a void" is a Pathosformel. "Sublime loneliness"
   is a theme. "Space that watches back" is a Pathosformel. "Liminality"
   is a theme. The test: can the sentence survive being applied to a work
   from a completely different subject domain? If yes, it is likely
   describing a posture; if no, it is probably describing a theme.

5. **Nachleben trails must cross a cultural or epochal boundary.** A
   single-century, single-culture trail is a school, not Nachleben.
   Both examples above span ≥100 years and ≥2 distinct cultural
   contexts (German Romanticism / Soviet late modernism / Hollywood;
   Dutch Golden Age / Danish symbolism / British war cinema).

6. **Mark proposals explicitly.** Any [[wikilink]] that does not resolve
   to an existing seed-kit file must be marked `[PROPOSAL: slug]`. Never
   imply that a proposed work already exists in the vault. Both examples
   use this marking consistently.

7. **Panel membership requires confirmation.** Neither example promotes
   a panel to full status without confirmed member works meeting
   `cluster_member_min: 3`. Where the threshold is unmet, the
   deficit is named explicitly.

## Anti-patterns these examples guard against

- **The theme-folder panel.** A panel named "Images of solitude" or
  "Threshold imagery" is a tag cloud, not a Bilderatlas panel. Both
  panels above (`[[sublime-solitude-landscape]]`,
  `[[liminal-threshold-zone]]`) are named after affective postures and
  spatial configurations, not subject categories.

- **Layer I contamination.** The single most common failure: writing
  "the monk stands contemplating the infinite" in a Layer I block. That
  sentence contains "monk" (Layer II, iconographic identification) and
  "contemplating the infinite" (Layer III, iconological attribution).
  Layer I cannot contain either. If this error appears, rewrite until
  only perceptual data remains.

- **The confidence inflation of Nachleben.** Writing "Villeneuve
  references Friedrich" or "Garland quotes Tarkovsky" mistakes Nachleben
  for citation. The posture survives through the visual unconscious;
  conscious quotation is a separate and weaker claim. Stronger phrasing:
  "the Rückenfigur posture *survives into* this frame."

- **Authority anchor invention.** If an AAT ID, Iconclass notation, or
  Wikidata QID is not verified, the uncertainty protocol applies:
  `aat_id_uncertainty: ["candidate-1", "candidate-2"]`. Never write a
  specific numeric ID without verifying it resolves to the correct term.
  Both examples above explicitly flag uncertain anchors rather than
  asserting them.

- **Closed-thesis prose.** An entry under this lens should leave
  juxtapositional traces — three works in a panel, the rupture noted, the
  affect named — not deliver a completed argument with premises and
  conclusion. If the entry reads like a finished essay, it has collapsed
  the space the method requires. The Mnemosyne Atlas had no captions;
  the configuration was the argument.
